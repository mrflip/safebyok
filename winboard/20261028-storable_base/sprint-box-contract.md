## Ground rules

1. **Who does this method serve?** We believe the control flow as you find it is sound and nearly complete; the exceptions are listed under *Where the spec and the code disagree*. A method that gathers lines sharing one concern under one name: good. A method that dries up repeated lines at the cost of a longer stack trace and a reader who now has to look elsewhere: very bad. Functionality the plan does not call for, a clever remedy for an edge condition, say: no thank you. Where you judge new workings necessary, be sure the code and its stack traces come out cleaner, give it a punchy verbNoun name (`spendStaged`, never an anodyne `takeValue` that could mean anything), and say in the thread file who it serves. No retry, no scheduling, no abort controllers, no policy knobs: the library offers simple brittle primitives (spec, *Please Remember!!*).
2. **Deviations from the old guarantees are findings, not work.** The model will not provide everything `notes/guarantees.md`, the README and the old `src/box.ts` provided. Two kinds, kept apart. A guarantee the Coach has firmly loosened is the contract now, tested in the ordinary files like any other row: the sidecar is not readable while locked; an operation that passed its guards completes without error even if the lock comes down before it returns; a healthy received record drops the staged. ## The absent contract

An absent thing, one deleted, damaged or never stored, answers as a property set to null does (the Coach, 2026-10-30). Reading it is no error where the reader says so: `fetch` refuses `absent_box` by default and resolves null under `ifAbsent: 'nil'`, which a thing's own `hydrate` always passes; `at` answers the instance held, as its tombstone; `sidecar` and the sealed parts read null; the snapshot says `present: false`. Carrying it somewhere keeps it absent: `build(tombstone)` holds a gone instance, the cache saves the tombstone, a listing that receives one holds one. Writing over it is no error: `edit` is its rebirth, `send` puts the record made, `del` deletes again. Using it is the error: `unsealWhisper`, and later anything that needs the whisper, refuses `absent_box`. `ifAbsent` is thread 3's. Where a gap shows up, the thread does the simplest thing, tests it, and writes it here; something nearly as simple and notably less absurd wins over the simplest.

## For the Coach

Edge cases met while making `registered_at` follow the record (2026-10-30), noted and not addressed:

* `build(dna)` of a scope already held makes a new instance and `#hold` replaces the held one, so one durable instance per scope does not survive a `build(dna)` over a tombstone or a live thing. The simplest cure is `build(dna)` refusing `bad_option` where `at(scope)` holds something, with `edit` as the way to rebirth; until you say, the behaviour is pinned in the loosened file.
* A tombstone keeps the `registered_at` of the record it buries, since `tombstoneFor` spreads central's sparse props; the ouchstone likewise takes central's, never the damaged record's. A thing deleted and reborn therefore shows the old `registered_at` until the rebirth is staged.
* A storage adapter that keeps the stored `registered_at` on a write over a tombstone, as a database default might, answers a record registered at the old time; the answer is newer, so it is adopted and the own prop follows it. The staged's `now` is then lost quietly. The fake store writes what it is given.
* The README's `putBox` keeps `registered_at` when writing over the revision the snapshot lists, and writes `{}` as the sidecar over a record that will not open; whether the old code kept or renewed `registered_at` over a damaged box is a describe for the loosened file once thread 4 has the behaviour.


* The seal's place in `edit` (the `fill` and `adopt` merge re-mints a revision without resealing) is out of this sprint and will surface in thread 2's findings; thread 6 lists the rows that wait on it.
* `ifAbsent` is thread 3's and `ifPresent` thread 8's, each as far as it is needed now.
