# Thread 11: graphql_manifest (2026-11-26)

Branch `20261126-graphql_manifest`, PR [#947](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/pull/947), stacked on [#946](https://github.com/mrflip/safebyok/pull/946). Proved: safebyok 2,450 tests, safebyok-graphql 295 (its battery over memory and Postgres among them), typecheck and lint green in both. `pnpm justify`: see the PR.

* **Built**
  * **`GraphQLVaultDB` on the manifest** (`packages/safebyok-graphql/src/graphql-vault-db.ts`). Each `do*` hands back, as its verb's manifest, what the server answered. The rows are those the answer carries. A shell (told by its revseq, -1) reads as no row; for a get or a write it also gives the ancestor's lives (below). The tags are those the server's act names where it names some: a get's or a listing's `presence` (`PresenceOfAct`; an act that names none, such as a get's `stale`, leaves it undefined and the base class's tag check refuses `storage_issue`), and whether a write or a birth wrote (`writeActVals`). Where the act names none, `ManifestTags` makes them of the rows, as `MemoryDatastore` does: an unwritten write by `writeTagsOf(ask, actual, false)` (its ante row is the row the server carried), a written one by `writtenTagsOf(ask)`, a birth by `birthTagsOf(puts, …)` over the actual roots, or over no roots where it wrote. A refused birth's roots put the root `dbhas` names at its labelpath and null at the others (`namedRootsOf`). The roots before it must have been null, or their puts would have passed or refused first, and the roots after it are never read. A listing's children are the answer's `deltas`, plus its `incompatible_rows` handed on as raw children. Those carry only their scope, so they fail every kind's check and come back as incompatible. Thrown refusals cross as before (`throwIfThrown`, `refusedOverWire`, `crossedErrOf`), keyed on `tracked: 'unread'`.
  * **Wire** (`shapes-wire.ts`, `vault-schema.ts`, `vault-operations.ts`, `vault-resolvers.ts`). Banktok, tracked, read_tc and every Thing are left raw for the base class to check. The listing answer drops `val` (the kids): the client makes them again from `parentage` and `deltas`. `damaged_rows` → `incompatible_rows`, `SafebyokDamagedThing` → `SafebyokIncompatibleThing`, and `SafebyokKids` is gone. A good listing's `parentage` must have exactly one entry (a `tuple`).
  * **Core loosenings** (`packages/safebyok`, commit `storage: a datastore may hand back a live box sparse…`). Both are needed because the server's answer is a lossy view of its manifest:
    * `ancestor` may be `AncestorLives`, `{ userid, banklabel, labelpath, lifepath }` and no other field: a strict check (`ManifestChecks.ancestorLives`, with `isLifepathDeep`) tried before the kind's `stored` check in `checkedAncestorOf`. A void get or write answers only the shell, which names the ancestor's lifepath and nothing else of it.
    * A listing's parent and children may be a live box's sparse form: each kind bundle gains `listed` (`BoxChecks.listed` = `boxThing` or `boxSparse`; the bank's and gate's equal `stored`), read by `checkedParentOf` and `checkedChildrenOf`. The listing answer carries live boxes sparse.
    * A whole row fails either strict check, so a datastore that hands back whole rows is checked as before. One exception: a stored box row missing both `sidecar` and `whisper_sealed` now lists as live where it was incompatible (a get of it still refuses).
  * **Deleted.** `CrossedChecks.tracked`, `.roots`, `.listing` and `.damagedThing`, and `refusedOverWire`'s `dbhas` (`CrossedAnswered` is `{ act? }`). No thrower in the new core sets `rows.dbhas`, so a server's thrown answer always carries null. Kept: `CrossedChecks.err` and `.answered`, `crossedErrOf`, `refusedOverWire`. `writeActVals` is added to `safebyok/storage`'s exports.
  * **security.md**: an entry (graphql_manifest) records the server-as-datastore checks, the refused act left to the client, and the two lossy forms.
  * **Tests.** `safebyok-graphql/tests/graphql-vault-db.test.ts` covers the dropped crossed tests' refusals through the manifest: a row at another address, a row of another kind (`incompatible_row`), a get or listing act its row does not fit, a shell that is no shell, a write or birth whose written-ness its row does not fit, a written row without the record, a birth's roots of another bank, missing or as a shell, a tracked with a root missing or not its own, a world root of another bank, a listing child in another bank, a grandchild, one of another kind, an incompatible row outside the parent, and an empty parentage. The core's `adapter-vault-db.test.ts` covers both loosenings.
* **Decisions made**
  * **The server's act becomes a tag only where it names one.** A get's act maps one to one onto `presence`, and whether a write wrote is the datastore's own observation, so a server whose act disagrees with its row refuses `storage_issue` (`tagsOfOtherRow`, `writtenOfOtherAct`, `proposedNotWritten`). A refused write's or birth's act (stale, retcon, clash…) names no single set of tags, so those tags are made of the row and the client answers the act its own tables find. **Set aside:** checking the server's act against the client's. That needs `ManifestActs`' act functions exported and read in `do*`, which the contract forbids a datastore, or a per-call hook in the base class. See *For the Coach*.
  * **Shell → ancestor in the client.** The shell is checked as a shell of any kind (`WireChecks.shell`). The ancestor's lifepath is the shell's stored lifeseqs, under as many labels of the ask's labelpath. A shell whose depth or lives disagree is refused by the shell check or by the core's `ancestorLives` check.
* **Deviations**
  * **Wire changed beyond the rename**: the listing's kids no longer cross. Nothing reads them now; keeping them meant a check of a field that is thrown away.
  * **Refusal kinds moved for a server out of step**: a row of another kind than the ask's (a box at the gate, the gate as a listing's parent) refuses `incompatible_row` where the crossed layer refused `storage_issue`, as for any datastore. A refused act alone out of step answers the client's act (above). A thrown refusal no longer names a Thing.
* **For the Coach**
  * **The core's two loosenings** change the datastore contract (`notes/vault-adapter-contract.md` says so). The alternative was to put the server's manifest on the wire: the resolvers would need it from the server's vaultDB, so `AdapterVaultDB` would have to expose its `do*` or a public manifest call, and `vaultResolvers` would take an `AdapterVaultDB` rather than any `VaultDB`.
  * **A refused act out of step with its row** answers the client's act instead of refusing. Recommendation: accept, since the client's act is right for the row the server carried.
