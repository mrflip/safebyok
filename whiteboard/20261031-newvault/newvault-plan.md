## Ground rules

1. **Only `Proving` and `Lease` touch cryptography.** They alone import a key maker, `wrapKeyUnder`, `unwrapKeyUnder`, `cipherBytes` or `decipherBytes`. An unwrapped key, a handle included, never leaves a local variable: no field, no return, no argument across a class boundary. The two exceptions are by design and already built: the vaultKey moves proving to proving inside `Proving` (`makeWayin(source)`), and `Proving.makeLease` hands it to `Lease.grant` as an argument. `Gate`, `Vault`, `Box` and the listings hold no key, ever. (The Coach, 2026-10-31; guarantees G3, G5, G9.)
2. **A ctx is built once, by its owner, in one small function.** The box builds its stamp, a flat bag, once; the lease turns a stamp into ctx bytes under the vault's stamp and its own purposes, and builds the one for its own key. No helper that takes a purpose and a method name and walks a chain (the old `boxCtxOf` was four calls deep to DRY two hashes). Never pass a function or method name around to be invoked on another instance.
3. **`try*` answers an `Outcome` `{ ok, val, err }`; the plain verb throws.** Throw through small factory functions (`damagedLease()`), as `storable.ts` does. *The Coach (2026-10-31):* the factories bring their sentences in from `phrases.ts` (`Phrases`), never inline, and may take objects they sanitize and attach (the two revisions that conflict, say); thread 2 moves the sentences the model files already hold into `phrases.ts`, the one old-`src` file every thread may touch for this. Catch what needs catching (a key that will not open is learned from the AEAD failure, answered as the fitting refusal through `outcomeOf(open, refusal)`), let the rest propagate; catch-and-rethrow is not forbidden outright, it just has to earn its lines and keep the page clean. The two host functions that hear the passphrase, the proof closure and the key deriver, relabel by design (G1, G8).
4. **Checkers, and the types made from them, live in `models-shapes.ts`.** A model file is code. (In `src/shapes/` normally; the models are held apart for now.)
5. **Key-handling code zeroes its byte arrays in `try/finally`**, whatever happens: whisper bytes once ciphered, PRF bytes once a key is derived, key bytes once imported.
6. **Loosened on purpose**: no consistency, timeliness or lock-state checks come back in this sprint, no retry logic, no abort controllers. Where the simplified code or the storable contract looks wrong to you, do not fix it: pin it as a test in `packages/safebyok/tests/loosened/` (the storable sprint's convention, `tests/loosened/loosened.test.ts`), named for what it pins, and list it in your thread file.
   *The Coach (2026-10-31), on the rollback guard:* "I'm not against all consistency checks -- its the old code's mania for them I'm against." A check that guards a real hazard may come back, one at a time, by ruling; the reflexive ones stay out.
7. **Old promises are not honoured.** The old library's refusal kinds, its `damaged_wayin` dance, its consistency demands and its gating objects are not models of anything here. Pre-1.0: no migrations, no reading of old stored shapes.
8. **Doc blocks short.** One or two lines per export, written for its caller; no `@example` essays. The `documentarian` passes later. *The Coach (2026-10-31):* short now, more later; but no slop of the shape "What the lease sends to the box; its crypto context when blah is the parameter, its other_thing standing to raise the foo". A line that narrates the parameters or restates the signature is stripped or fixed by the worker that meets it and by the reviewer after, and never written anew. Tests come in threads 5 and 6; a code thread adds a test only where it is cheap and pins a decision.
9. **Names**: VOCABULARY.md's; `read`/`write` are category words, never names; name the operation (`Lease.get`, `vault.lock()`). The friend a lease gets is the `dbfriend`. The stored lease field is `autolock_at`.
10. **Stay in `models/`** and its tests, plus `models-shapes.ts`, the spec, and a one-line export where a thread says. Old `src/` files are not edited.
