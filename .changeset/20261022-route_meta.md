"safebyok-postgres": minor

**The Postgres route recipe answers every call in one shape, and a refusal no longer carries a record's wrapped keys or ciphertext.** The recipe's route (README, *The route recipe*) answers `{ result, meta }` when a call was served and `{ refusal: { kind, message, meta }, meta }` when it was refused. The main `meta` is on every answer and holds what a write learned about what stands: a stale box write's `box_stored`, a stale update's `vault_props_stored` and a full vault's `capacity` (nothing on a served call, so `meta: {}`). The refusal's own `meta` holds error information only, and is rebuilt by the new `answerOf`, `boxShown` and `wayinShown` from the fields a locked vault's snapshot already shows: a damaged box's `boxlabel`, `damaged` and `revision_tok`, a wayin's `wayinkind`, `device_id`, `registered_at`, `vault_tok` and (a passkey's) `credential_id` and `prf_salt_b64`. Before, the route passed on whatever `err.meta` held, so a refusal that listed a record could carry its wrapped vaultKey, its ciphertext or a passphrase wayin's salt to the browser and into whatever logs the browser's traffic. The client half's `checkedAnswer` believes an answer only whole (a `meta` in its place, a `result` or a `refusal` and not both, a refusal of a known kind) and refuses another as `storage_unavailable`, then rethrows the refusal's `VaultError` with both metas merged, so a vault's code and the VaultDB conformance suite are unchanged. **A host that copied the recipe copies it again, and redeploys its route and its app together** (no compatibility before 1.0): an older route's refusals carry `box_stored`, `vault_props_stored` and `capacity` in the refusal's `meta` and no main `meta`, which the new browser refuses as `storage_unavailable`; a new route's stale write read by the older browser lacks them and refuses `corrupt_record`. `safebyok-graphql` keeps its own wire.
