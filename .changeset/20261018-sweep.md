The vocabulary reform, in one place: this release renames what a host touches, and what a vault stores, to the words VOCABULARY.md defines. Nothing has a compatibility alias, and nothing stored before it is read. The entries above this one say each change in full; this is what a host does.

Names. The unlock envelope is the wayin; what a device keeps to unlock again is the lease (`forcePassphraseReentryHours` is `leaseHours`); a lockbox's public bag is its sidecar; the bundle is the vault props; erase is retire (`store`, `read`, `use`, `erase` and `destroy` are `storeBox`, `readBox`, `useBox`, `retireBox` and `retire`); `revision_id` is `revision_tok`; the encryption's "binding" is its ctx. Every renamed export, option, stored field, ckey and refusal kind (`absent_wayin`, `damaged_wayin`, `last_wayin`, `too_many_wayins`, `damaged_vault`) is in the *Old names* table of VOCABULARY.md, beside its replacement; a DeviceDB or VaultDB of your own renames its methods to match.

Making a vault. `openVault(opts)` is gone, with no alias: write `const vault = Vault.make(opts)`, which returns at once, and `await vault.hello()` where you want to know the vault has settled. `Vault.make` now refuses an option it does not take as `bad_option`, naming it (and the new name of a retired one), where it used to ignore it; VaultProvider throws a prop it does not take to its error boundary the same way. A hello that cannot read storage leaves the vault `opening` with the snapshot's new `trouble` saying why, and any later call tries again.

Behaviour. `storeBox()` no longer retries when another device wrote first: it catches up once and throws `stale_write` at once, for your app to decide whether to store again. A first unlock that loses the race to another device creating the vault throws the new `created_elsewhere` in place of `wrong_passphrase`: a host that says "check for typos" on `wrong_passphrase` should handle it too (safebyok-react's PassphraseModal shows it as advice).

Stored formats. The vault props are `safebyok-vault-v3` (was `-v2`), a lockbox `safebyok-lockbox-v5` (was `-v4`) and a device's lease `safebyok-lease-v1` (was `safebyok-unlock-v1`, filed under `lease^` rather than `unlock^` in the IndexedDB device store). There is no migration, and an old record fails safe: a vault written before reads as damaged (`damaged_vault`) and is retired with `retireDamagedVault()` and made afresh, a lockbox written before lists as damaged and is stored again or retired, and a lease written before is dropped unread, so each device asks for the passphrase or passkey once. The lockbox's whisper has to be stored again by the user.

Postgres. Run these, in this order, once, on an existing database, before this release's `VaultTablesSql` or `VaultNotifySql` first runs (run first, they would make an empty `safebyok_vaults` beside the old table, and the renames would then fail):

```sql
ALTER TABLE safebyok_bundles RENAME COLUMN unlocks TO wayins;
ALTER TABLE safebyok_lockboxes RENAME COLUMN public_json TO sidecar_json;
ALTER TABLE safebyok_bundles RENAME TO safebyok_vaults;
ALTER TRIGGER safebyok_bundles_changed ON safebyok_vaults RENAME TO safebyok_vaults_changed;
ALTER TABLE safebyok_lockboxes RENAME COLUMN revision_id TO revision_tok;
```

Convex. The component's tables and validators changed (`safebyok_vaults`, `wayins`, `sidecar_json`, `revision_tok`, `vault_row_id`), so a deployment holding rows written before this no longer matches the component's schema: clear the component's tables before deploying it, and regenerate your app's `api`.

GraphQL. The schema's types and operations follow (`SafebyokVaultProps`, `SafebyokWayin`, `safebyokReadVaultProps`, `safebyokRetireBox` and the rest): serve the new schema and the new client together.
