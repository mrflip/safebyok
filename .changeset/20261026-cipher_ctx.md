**Stored format: every encryption binds stamps, and nothing sealed before opens.** Each ctx is now a thing's **stamp** (the fields of its record fixed for its life), its purpose, and a wayin's visible header. A wayin's wrap, and a passkey's key, bind its vault record's `VaultStamp`, its `registered_at` now among it. A box's two ciphered fields bind the vault's stamp without its `format` (the scope, `vault_tok` and `registered_at`), beside the box's own `BoxStamp`; a lease's wrap binds the vault's stamp without its `format`, beside the lease's own `LeaseStamp`. So a later vault format leaves every box and lease open. The formats stay as they were (`safebyok-vault-v6`, `safebyok-box-v8`, `safebyok-lease-v1`), and no wayin, box or lease sealed before this release opens. This release already reads every vault stored before it as damaged (the nested vault record), so a host sees no new damage: `delDamagedVault` clears the vault and the lease of the device it runs on, and the user makes the vault anew. A lease left on another device names the old vault's `vault_tok`, so it is never taken up: that device asks for the passphrase, and its next unlock writes over it.

**Exports.** `Wayin.make(vault, vaultStamp, record)`, and the `PassphraseWayin` and `PasskeyWayin` constructors, take the stamp of the vault record the wayin is one of where they took its `vault_tok`: the vault record itself will do, as only its six stamp fields are taken, and one missing any refuses `bad_record`. `WayinVault` is `keyDeriver` and `now` alone; `GateVault` names the scope it reads; `LeaseVault` adds `vault_tok` and `registered_at`, which a lease's and a box's ctx take from the vault. `raisedDeviceRecord`'s doc block now says it throws `bad_record` rather than raise a stored record to an epoch that fails its check, which no record the core hands a DeviceDB carries.
