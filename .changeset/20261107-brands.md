safebyok and safebyok/storage export `brand(obj, Brand, val = true)` and `brandOf(val, Brand)`, which mark an object with a hidden `Symbol.for` key and read it back, and `IsVaultError`, the brand every VaultError wears. `isVaultError` now accepts a VaultError from a second copy of safebyok (an ESM and a CJS build side by side, or one an adapter bundles); one rebuilt from JSON still fails. **`box.exposure`** is new: where the whisper stands, read live, as `'unexposed'`, `'locked'`, `'veiled'` or `'showing'` (the exported `BoxExposure` and `boxExposureVals`). A host learns why a whisper hides from it, from `box.isShowing`, the new `box.isVeiled` (true while veiled, even behind a lock) or `isWhisperedBag`, and from the snapshot's `isExposed` and `isVeiled` beside the vault snapshot's `isLocked`. It does not compare `box.whisper` with `ENCRYPTED`, `LOCKED` or `VEILED`, which the front door now exports, nor read their `Whispered` mark: a second copy of safebyok has bags of its own. **Dropping the whisper drops the veil**: after `box.unexpose()`, a delete or a tombstone received, a sweep or a vacate, `box.isVeiled` and the snapshot's `isVeiled` read false; `veil()` on a box not exposed still refuses nothing. safebyok-react's `useWhisper` answers the core's `ENCRYPTED` for no box, and its new **`useBoxExposure(box)`** follows `box.exposure` (`'unexposed'` for no box); `useBoxIsVeiled`, which no release carried, is gone, as the box snapshot's `isVeiled` says the same. safebyok-graphql's `vaultResolvers` knows a VaultDB by its brand, so one from a second copy of safebyok passes.
