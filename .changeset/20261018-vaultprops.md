The bundle is now the vault's props, and erase is now retire, everywhere; retire still removes for good, with no tombstone. Exports: VaultBundle is VaultProps, BundleExpectation is VaultPropsExpectation, EraseExpectation is RetireExpectation, EraseDamagedVaultOpts is RetireDamagedVaultOpts, eraseDamagedVault() is retireDamagedVault(), Ckeys.bundle is Ckeys.vaultProps (the stored ckey is unchanged), Refusals.staleWrite('bundle' | 'erase') is staleWrite('vaultProps' | 'retire'), and safebyok/testing's bundleOf and rotatedBundleOf are vaultPropsOf and rotatedVaultPropsOf. On the vault, store(), read(), use(), erase() and destroy() are storeBox(), readBox(), useBox(), retireBox() and retire(), and the logger's story names those calls the same way. On a VaultDB, readBundle, createBundle and updateBundle are readVaultProps, fetchOrCreateVaultProps and updateVaultProps (same meaning: it returns the vault props standing afterwards, its own or another device's), eraseBox, eraseVault and eraseDamagedVault are retireBox, retireVault and retireDamagedVault, and an adapter's abstract doReadBundle, doCreateBundle, doUpdateBundle, doEraseBox and doEraseVault are doReadVaultProps, doFetchOrCreateVaultProps, doUpdateVaultProps, doRetireBox and doRetireVault: a VaultDB of your own renames its methods. The refusal kind damaged_bundle is damaged_vault, and several refusal messages now say retire. Stored: a lockbox's revision_id is revision_tok, in the summary, in DamagedLockbox and in a LockboxExpectation. This rides the format bumps already in this release (safebyok-vault-v3, safebyok-lockbox-v5): no second bump, and the lockbox's ctx follows the renamed field, so a lockbox written before this release lists as damaged and is stored again or retired. Postgres: VaultTablesSql and VaultNotifySql create only what is missing, so an existing database runs ALTER TABLE safebyok_bundles RENAME TO safebyok_vaults; ALTER TRIGGER safebyok_bundles_changed ON safebyok_vaults RENAME TO safebyok_vaults_changed; ALTER TABLE safebyok_lockboxes RENAME COLUMN revision_id TO revision_tok -- in that order, and before this release's VaultTablesSql or VaultNotifySql first runs: run first, they would make an empty safebyok_vaults beside the old table, every vault would read as never created, and the rename would then fail. (The table's keys and constraints keep their safebyok_bundles_ names, which nothing reads.) GraphQL: SafebyokVaultBundle(Input) is SafebyokVaultProps(Input), SafebyokBundleExpectationInput is SafebyokVaultPropsExpectationInput, SafebyokEraseExpectationInput is SafebyokRetireExpectationInput, the operations safebyokReadBundle, safebyokCreateBundle, safebyokUpdateBundle, safebyokEraseBox and safebyokEraseVault are safebyokReadVaultProps, safebyokFetchOrCreateVaultProps, safebyokUpdateVaultProps, safebyokRetireBox and safebyokRetireVault, and their bundle argument is vaultProps: serve the new schema and the new client together. Convex: the component's functions getBundle, createBundle, updateBundle, eraseBox and eraseVault are getVaultProps, fetchOrCreateVaultProps, updateVaultProps, retireBox and retireVault (their bundle argument is vaultProps), its table safebyok_bundles is safebyok_vaults, and the tally's bundle_id and by_bundle are vault_row_id and by_vault_row, so regenerate your app's api and, as a deployment holding rows written before this no longer matches the component's schema, clear the component's tables before deploying it. React: the boundary and provider speak of damaged_vault, and the start-over flow calls vault.retire(). There is no migration.
