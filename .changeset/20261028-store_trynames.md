**The storage classes' verbs are try pairs, named for what they resolve.** Every public verb of `VaultDB` (`getVault`, `ensureVault`, `getVaultRecords`, `ensureVaultRecords`, `putVaultRecord`, `getBox`, `putBox`, `getCapacity`, `delBox`, `delVault`, `delDamagedVault`) and of `DeviceDB` (`getLease`, `putLease`, `delLease`, `getDeviceRecord`, `raiseDeviceRecord`, `delDeviceRecord`, `delAll`) used to resolve an `Outcome`. Now each name resolves its answer or throws its refusal, and its `try` twin (`tryGetVault`, `tryEnsureVault`, `tryGetVaultRecords`, `tryEnsureVaultRecords`, `tryPutVaultRecord`, `tryGetBox`, `tryPutBox`, `tryGetCapacity`, `tryDelBox`, `tryDelVault`, `tryDelDamagedVault`; `tryGetLease`, `tryPutLease`, `tryDelLease`, `tryGetDeviceRecord`, `tryRaiseDeviceRecord`, `tryDelDeviceRecord`, `tryDelAll`) resolves the `Outcome` it used to. A host calling a store's verb for its `Outcome` renames the call to the `try` form; one unwrapping it with `valOf` calls the throwing form instead. The `do*` hooks a subclass implements do not change; an adapter handing a call on to another VaultDB calls that VaultDB's throwing verb (`inner.getBox`) where it unwrapped with `valOf`. The refusals, their kinds and their order are as they were; a `DeviceDB`'s throwing verb throws the same `VaultError` its `try` form resolves as `err`, a store's throw made `storage_blocked` or `storage_unavailable` as every verb did. `describeVaultDB`'s `VaultDBVerbs` (from `safebyok/testing`) names the `try` forms; `DeviceVerb` names the throwing forms. **Stored format:** unchanged.
