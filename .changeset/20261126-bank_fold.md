A bank is no longer a stored Thing of its own: its gate is stored at the labelpath that is its banklabel (for banklabel `acme`, at `acme`), and carries the bank's `banktok`; the gate and the root box are the bank's two roots. No vault stored before this reads after it, and there is no migration: a bank whose old root box survives cannot be born again, since its birth refuses `stale`. Clear the old data before upgrading:

* **safebyok-postgres**: drop `safebyok_bank_things`, `safebyok_gate_things` and `safebyok_box_things` and every `safebyok_a010501_r000_*` function, then run `VaultTablesSql` and `VaultFunctionsSql` again (`VaultTablesSql` creates only what is missing, and Postgres will not replace a function whose arguments or result changed). A host that LISTENs on the channel itself gets, under `bank`, the gate's `banktok`, `labelpath`, `lifepath`, `revseq` and `revtok`, where it got the bank's row.
* **safebyok-convex**: clear `bank_things`, `gate_things` and `box_things` on the dashboard's Data page (pick the `safebyok` component) before deploying. The schema drops `bank_things`, which Convex refuses while it has rows, and every table gains `banktok`, which an old document lacks. The component's `getWorld` query is `getBank`.
* **safebyok-indexeddb**: nothing to clear. Its Things move to the database `safebyok-<dbName>:vault-v002-001-001`, each keyed by its scope, kind first, `[modelkind, userid, banklabel, labelpath]`, and the database before it (`…:vault-v001-001-001`) is deleted unread at first use, so each user's bank reads as unborn.
* **safebyok-graphql**: deploy this version's `VaultSchemaSdl` and `vaultResolvers` on the server together with the new client: an old client fails the new schema's validation, and an old server answers a new client with `unexpected_error`. `safebyokGetWorld` is `safebyokGetBank`, and `VaultOperations.getWorld` is `getBank`.

`device.bank` is always there, made with the device, and `device.world` is gone. Where code checked it for null, ask `bank.areRootsLive` or the snapshot's `stage`. safebyok-react's `useWorld` is `useBank`, and its `SignedOutWorld` is `SignedOutBank`.

Renamed for anyone implementing or faking a `VaultDB`, or reading its answers: `tryGetWorld` is `tryGetBank` (`doGetWorld`, `doGetBank`), `GetWorldAsk` is `GetBankAsk` with op `'getbank'`, `GetWorldAnswer` is `GetBankAnswer`, `WorldManifest` is `GetBankManifest`, and `WorldRoots` is `ActualRoots`. `WorldSnapshot` is `BankSnapshot`, `CarWorld` is `CarBank`, `Labelpath.MainGate` is `Labelpath.gateOf(banklabel)`, and `RootLabelpaths` is `rootLabelpathsOf(scope)`.

Every Thing's record states its kind, `modelkind` (`'gate'` or `'box'`), and a Thing's kind is read from its record, its ask or the table it is kept in, never from its labelpath. So a bank may be labelled `box`: its gate and its root box both sit at `box`, each in its own kind's table. A gate's and a box's stamps bind the `modelkind`.

* **safebyok-postgres**: each table has a `modelkind` column, which a `CHECK` fixes to its kind. A notification's `at` carries the Thing's `modelkind` beside its scope.
* **safebyok-convex**: each table's documents have a `modelkind` field, fixed to its kind. `watchThings` hands back each Thing's `modelkind` beside its labelpath.
* **safebyok-graphql**: `SafebyokNotice` has a `modelkind`, and `VaultOperations.watchThings` selects it.

For anyone implementing or faking a `VaultDB`: a watch's `Received` carries the `modelkind` of the table written; `vaultStatsOf` takes each kind's rows apart, `{ gate, box }`; and a test's row cheats (`VaultDBRowCheats.plant`, `remove`) take a `ThingScope`. `Labelpath.modelkindOf`, `Labelpath.isOfModelkind` and `ThingkindChecks.ofLabelpath` are gone; `isLabelpathOfKind` holds where a labelpath follows its kind's rule.

A Thing's scope, `ThingScope`, is its `modelkind`, `userid`, `banklabel` and `labelpath`, kind first, and names one Thing; a bank labelled `box` has its gate and its root box at two scopes. Every call that took a Thing's scope takes the `modelkind` with it: the vault TSQ's `itemQueryOptions`, `getOnce`, `itemQueryKeyOf` and `listQueryKeyOf`, and the VaultMinder's `carAt`, `thingAt`, `tryGetOnce`, `getOnce`, `tryListOnce` and `listOnce`. A `TableScope` is a scope less its labelpath, the Things of one kind in one bank, and `rootScopesOf(scope)` gives each root's scope. A VaultMinder listener's `onBadNews` is told the refused record's scope, where it was told its labelpath. `BaseThing.haveSameScope` compares the kinds too. An `IncompatibleThing` among a listing's `incompatible_rows` carries the `modelkind`, as does safebyok-graphql's `SafebyokIncompatibleThing`, which `VaultOperations.listThings` selects. The query keys the vault TSQ makes start `['sok', modelkind, userid, banklabel]`, where they started `['sok', userid, banklabel]`, so a default set on `['sok', 'box']` reaches every box's item and listing; `VaultTSQ.bankQueryKeyOf` is `tableQueryKeyOf`, and the vault TSQ's defaults sit under `['sok', 'box', userid, banklabel]`. A host that built a box's key by hand builds it with the kind. safebyok-react's keys follow, its signed-out keys starting `['sok', 'box', null, …]`; its form mutations' keys are as they were.

A bank's end is its gate's tombstone, which keeps the bank's `banktok` and no wayin: its `wayins` are `{ main: null, passkey0: null }`, and a live gate's `main` is a wayin, so a gate row of any other shape reads as `incompatible_row`. A birth over a bank whose gate, root box or both are tombstones writes each root's next life, where it refused `stale`. A bank whose gate and root box are both tombstones is `unborn`, where it was a broken state. Each birth mints a new `banktok`, its first and each over a tombstone, and a device holding the gate's tombstone asks with that tombstone's banktok, where it asked with the void banktok. `vault.bank` answers the device's bank whatever its stage, where it refused `absent_thing` until the bank's roots were live.

For anyone implementing or faking a `VaultDB`: a `BirthBankAsk` carries `cited: { gate, root }`, what each root's put cites, the shell or a tombstone, and each put is judged as any put is. `isBirthWritable(puts, ante)` holds where `isWritable` holds of both puts, a birth's tags for each root are a write's less `written`, and `ManifestTags.writtenBirthTagsOf(puts)` makes a written birth's. `track` and `tracked` key the root box `root`, where they keyed it `box`, and `TrackedRows` is `RootRows`. `Bank.mintLife` takes the gate a birth finds (its tombstone, or its shell), and `Gate.makeRecord` takes what the birth cites. A refused `tryBirthBank`'s `dbhas` is the bank's two roots as its step read them, `{ gate, root }`, each a record, a tombstone or the shell, as `tryGetBank`'s `val` is, where it was the one root that refused; where the verb threw, it is null.

* **safebyok-postgres**: `birth_roots` takes four arguments, each root's record and what its put cites: drop the two-argument function before running `VaultFunctionsSql`. `safebyok_gate_things`'s `CHECK` on `wayins` now admits a tombstone's, each place null; the tables are made again as above.
* **safebyok-graphql**: `SafebyokBirthBankAsk` has `cited`. `SafebyokRootsAnswer`'s `dbhas` is a `SafebyokRoots`, where it was `SafebyokJson`, and `VaultOperations.birthBank` and `getBank` select `dbhas { gate root }`.

Every Thing stores a `banktok`, the token of the bank's life it was written in: a gate's is minted at each birth (it was the gate's `lifetok`), and a box's is its gate's when it was sealed. Each encryption is bound to one stamp, where it was bound to the bank's stamp and the thing's own: a gate's and a box's stamp is its Thing fields but `label` and `lifeseq`, `banktok` among them; a wayin's is its gate's scope, `lifepath`, `born_at` and `banktok` and its own `label`; a lease's carries its gate's life and revtok under `gate_` names (`gate_labelpath`, `gate_lifepath`, `gate_born_at`, `gate_revtok`), where it carried `bank_lifeseq`, `bank_born_at`, `bank_lifetok` and `gate_lifeseq`. `ThingFormat` and `LeaseFormat` are `'a010502'`. No box, wayin or lease written before reads after: clear the data as above, and a device's stowed lease is dropped (IndexedDB's lease database moves to `…:lease-v002-001-001`, keyed `['lease', userid, banklabel, labelpath]`, and the database before it is deleted, the devicetok it kept for each user with it), so each browser asks the user to unlock once, and mints each user a new devicetok.

* **safebyok-postgres**: both tables have a `banktok` column; the functions are named `safebyok_a010502_r000_*`, and `tell_thing` takes the gate's row as its second argument, so drop the `safebyok_a010501_r000_*` functions as above. A notification's bank part is as above, dropped with the Thing when too large.
* **safebyok-convex**: `box_things` gains `banktok` too.
* **safebyok-graphql**: a Thing crosses whole, so the schema is unchanged by this.

Renamed or reshaped in `safebyok` for anyone building on the models: `lifetok` is `banktok` (`Lifetok` is `Banktok`, `VoidLifeTok` is `VoidBanktok`, the refusal tag `LifetokMoved` is `BanktokMoved`, the tag `lifetok_cmp` is `banktok_cmp`); `BankStamp` and `bank.stamp` are gone; `GateLife` and `gate.life` are what a wayin's and a lease's stamp take of the gate; `WayinStamp` and `ProvableGate` are new; `StampedGate` and `GateRevisionedStamp` are gone; `wayinCtxOf` takes the gate's life; `LeaseScope` and a lease record carry `modelkind: 'lease'`. The vault TSQ sets no mutation defaults, and no longer asks a `QueryClient` for `setMutationDefaults`: a box write takes its options from `VaultTSQ.mutationOptionsOf`.
