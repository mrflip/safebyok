A bank swept or vacated is spent: its snapshot reads `refused` with a `disabled` err, `hello`, `ensureVault` and `vaultAt` refuse `disabled`, and `bank.isEnabled` turns false for good; make another bank with `Bank.make`. `bank.vacate()` now answers a promise, which settles once the bank's leases on this device are erased; that erase still calls the host's `onPanic` and `onBadNews`, and only then is the bank disabled. A disabled bank calls none of the host's `onPanic`, `onBadNews` or `requestCleartext`, falling back to the core's defaults.

**A revoked identity is a panic, not bad news.** Where the vaultDB refuses `identity_revoked`, the bank's `onPanic` hears it, no longer its `onBadNews`, and the caller still gets the refusal. `DefaultOnPanic` vacates the bank on it, the bank's snapshot then reading `refused` with that refusal as its `err`, and still reports it to the platform. A host's own `onPanic` decides for itself: call `DefaultOnPanic(panic)` from it to keep the vacate. `onPanic` now hears anything the host must act on for safety, where it heard only a cached lease that outlived its erase. The exported `Panic` type gains `bank`, the bank it reached; a host that builds a `Panic` by hand adds it. `bank.vacate` takes the refusal that called for it, optional, and a repeat vacate answers the first one's promise. The new exported type `BankPanic` is how a vault or a listing panics through its bank.
